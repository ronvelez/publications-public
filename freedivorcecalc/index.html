Decisions people often still talk through

These are notes for later conversations. Skip any line that does not fit. You do not have to settle them today. This page does not choose an answer.

  • The house is often kept by one person, with a buyout of the other share, or sold so the proceeds can be divided. Who stays in the home while that is still open is its own question, and so is who pays the joint bills during that wait.
  • Health coverage usually needs a plan: which parent’s insurance covers the children, and how a spouse who has been on the other person’s plan will be covered. When you know a new premium, it belongs in the solo expenses in section 3.
  • Some people work out the terms with a mediator. Some each hire a lawyer. Some leave open questions to a judge. Section 6 can open a mediator or lawyer search when you want one.
  • The tax year of a divorce often raises two questions: whether the last return is filed together or separately, and who claims the children. An accountant or another tax professional is the person for that year. This page does not prepare a return.
  • Retirement accounts are often divided by a special court order. Taking money out early can bring taxes and a penalty. A gift, an inheritance, or something owned before the marriage is sometimes treated as one person’s property.
  • Pets, college costs for older children, and who keeps a car for work or for parenting come up often. Name them when they apply.
  • Joint credit cards and automatic payments can keep charging one person. The section 8 list includes a free credit report, which shows the open lines. Who may use those accounts during the wait is worth deciding on purpose.
  • Life-insurance beneficiaries, a will, and a power of attorney often still name a spouse. A lawyer can say when those may be changed. Changing them too soon can conflict with a later order.
  • A long marriage, a pension, or military retirement is a reason to ask a professional about benefits this page does not estimate.
