A plain map of what often happens

States write different rules. A judge, a mediator, or a written agreement decides the real outcome. This is only the shape of the path, so the blank space has some edges.

  1. You are still deciding, or you have decided and need a first picture. This page is for that stage. You can learn before you tell anyone.
  2. People usually gather income, bills, debts, accounts, and papers about the home. Section 6 is a starter list. One item today is a real start.
  3. Child support and spousal support follow your state’s rules. Some states publish a formula. Many leave spousal support to a judge. Section 2 links to an outside child-support calculator and a plain-language spousal-support guide. You can type a monthly figure back here and see it beside rent, food, and other bills. The boxes on this page do not calculate the guideline.
  4. The house, retirement, and debts usually come later, in the property conversation. Section 3 only subtracts a mortgage from a home value. It does not divide the house or say who should stay.
  5. Many people then talk with a family mediator, a family lawyer, or a certified divorce financial analyst. Parenting time, support, and housing get worked out in that process. Section 4 can open those searches when you choose. This page does not contact them.
